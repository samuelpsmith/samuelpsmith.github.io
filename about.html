About + curriculum vitae

Chemistry is the question. Spectroscopy is how I listen.

I am a doctoral researcher, instrument builder, programmer, and educator based in Albuquerque, New Mexico.

My research asks how molecular structure controls what happens after a molecule absorbs light. I approach that question through magnetic circular dichroism, femtosecond transient absorption, electronic-structure calculations, and the software needed to connect those measurements.

In Jeffrey Rack's research group at the University of New Mexico, I have redeveloped polarization-sensitive instrumentation, rebuilt pump-probe acquisition workflows, investigated magneto-optical activity in porphyrins, and studied photochemical inversion in chiral sulfoxides.

I also treat software and teaching as part of the research process. The Python tools I develop are used for instrument control, spectral processing, fitting, and visualization, while my teaching and mentoring span inorganic chemistry, advanced synthesis, mass spectrometry, and undergraduate research.
